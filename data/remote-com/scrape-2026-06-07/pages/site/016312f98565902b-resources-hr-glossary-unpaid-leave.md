# Unpaid leave

## What is unpaid leave?

Unpaid leave, also known as unpaid time off, refers to an approved absence from work where the employee does not receive pay for the time they are away. Unlike paid vacation or sick leave, unpaid leave allows employees to take time off without using their paid time off (PTO) balance or receiving compensation.

Unpaid time off matters because it offers flexibility for both employees and employers. For workers, it can support personal needs such as family emergencies, extended illness, education, or travel. For employers, it provides a structured way to support work-life balance while managing labor costs—especially when paid leave isn’t available or appropriate.

## Why unpaid leave matters to businesses

Unpaid leave allows businesses to maintain a positive and accommodating work culture without incurring additional costs. It is often used when employees need more time off than their paid leave allows, such as during parental leave extensions, sabbaticals, or medical leave that exceeds statutory or company-provided benefits.

While not legally required in all cases, unpaid leave may be mandated under certain conditions. For example, in the U.S., the Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) allows eligible employees to take up to 12 weeks of unpaid, job-protected leave for specified family and medical reasons. Employers must stay informed about applicable leave laws to remain compliant.

Providing the option for unpaid time off also helps businesses retain talent. It gives employees the assurance that they can address personal needs without risking their employment. A transparent unpaid leave policy, clearly outlined in the employee handbook, is key to preventing misunderstandings and maintaining productivity.

## Key considerations for managing unpaid leave

Effectively managing unpaid leave involves balancing employee needs with business continuity. Employers should create a clear leave policy that defines eligibility, approval processes, notice periods, and how unpaid time off impacts benefits, job status, and return-to-work procedures.

It's also important to document unpaid leave agreements to avoid confusion, especially in relation to accruals, healthcare coverage, and job protection. Managers should work with HR to evaluate staffing impacts and plan coverage during extended absences.

Key considerations include:

- Unpaid leave allows approved time off without pay.

- Supports employee flexibility without increasing payroll costs.

- May be required under laws like FMLA or similar regulations.

- Clear policies help manage expectations and avoid legal issues.

- Impacts on benefits, seniority, or PTO accrual should be communicated.

- Planning for temporary coverage is essential to ensure business continuity.
