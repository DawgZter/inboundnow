# What is a pay stub?

## What is a pay stub?

A “pay stub” is a document provided by an employer to an employee to detail earnings for a specific pay period and the year-to-date. It usually contains gross pay, deductions, and net pay.
Let's break down these terms:
Gross Pay: This is the total amount of money an employee earns before any deductions are made. Gross pay might include hourly wages or a salary, overtime, bonuses, and any other additional compensations.
Deductions: These are amounts subtracted from the gross pay, often involving taxes (such as federal, state, and local), social security, medicare, and potentially other expenses like health insurance premiums, retirement contributions, or union dues.
Net Pay: This is the amount the employee actually receives after all deductions. It's typically referred to as the “take-home pay”.
A pay stub provides transparency and fosters trust between the employer and the employee by providing clear, tangible evidence of payment. It's an invaluable tool for HR leaders to track wages, manage payroll , and resolve disputes or discrepancies.
In essence, a pay stub is a vital part of the record-keeping that contributes to the smooth functioning of any business, large or small. Moreover, employees typically need pay stubs for personal reasons like obtaining credit, applying for loans, or proving employment. Therefore, as an HR leader, ensuring accurate and timely issuance is essential.
Pay stubs go by different names in different countries. In the UK, for example, it's known as a “payslip”. In Australia and New Zealand, the term “pay advice” is sometimes used. Knowing these regional terms is advantageous for HR leaders managing global teams. It aids in maintaining effective communication and setting the right expectations, particularly important when addressing employee queries related to payroll.
