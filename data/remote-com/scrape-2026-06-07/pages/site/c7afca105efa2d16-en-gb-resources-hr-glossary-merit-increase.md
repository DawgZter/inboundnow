# What is a merit increase?

## What is a merit increase?

A merit increase — also known as a merit raise — is an adjustment to an employee's pay that recognises and rewards their individual performance and contributions to the company.
Unlike general cost-of-living adjustments or seniority-based raises, merit increases are directly tied to the employee's job performance, accomplishments, and ability to meet or exceed set goals and expectations.

## What is a typical merit increase?

A typical merit increase can vary, but it generally ranges from 2% to 5% of an employee's base pay. The exact percentage depends on several factors, including the employee's performance level, the company’s financial health, industry norms, and the overall compensation strategy of the organisation.

### What is a merit increase average?

The merit increase average refers to the typical percentage by which salaries are increased across an organisation or within a specific industry due to merit raises. This average can vary widely depending on economic conditions, industry standards, company performance, and budget constraints.

## What influences merit increases?

Merit increases can be influenced by a wide range of factors, including:

- Performance evaluations. Merit increases are often determined during annual or semi-annual performance reviews, where managers assess employees' performance against predefined goals and criteria.

- Budget constraints. The availability of funds within the company’s budget can impact the overall pool of money allocated for merit increases.

- Market and industry rates. Organisations may consider industry pay benchmarks and market rates to ensure their compensation remains competitive.

- Company performance. The overall financial health and performance of the company can influence the size and distribution of merit increases.

## Best practices for merit increases

- Be transparent and clearly communicate the criteria (and process) for awarding merit increases to your people.

- Apply merit increase policies consistently across your organisation to avoid perceptions of bias or favouritism.

- Maintain thorough documentation of performance evaluations and the rationale for merit increases to support decisions and address any disputes.
