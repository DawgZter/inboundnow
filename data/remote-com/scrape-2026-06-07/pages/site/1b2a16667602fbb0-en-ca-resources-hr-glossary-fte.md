# What is FTE?

FTE stands for full-time equivalent, a unit that indicates the workload of an employed person in a way that makes workloads comparable across various contexts. It is often used to measure a worker's involvement in a project, or to track cost allocations in an organisation. An FTE of 1.0 is equivalent to a full-time worker, while an FTE of 0.5 signals that the worker is only half-time.

Effective use of FTE in a company helps in precise staffing, which makes sure that every department or project has enough human resources to function efficiently without overstaffing, which can lead to unnecessary expenses. By calculating the FTE of part-time and temporary workers, companies can gain a clear view of total staffing needs and costs, aiding in more accurate budgeting and resource allocation.

To develop and implement an FTE strategy effectively, a company should start by defining the standard number of working hours that equates to 1.0 FTE, which typically aligns with full-time working hours. Management should then train department heads on how to calculate FTE for their teams, especially in scenarios involving part-time or irregular hours. Regular audits and updates of FTE calculations should be conducted to ensure they reflect any changes in staffing or operational needs.

## Key considerations for FTE

- Define a clear standard for what constitutes 1.0 FTE in your organisation.

- Ensure accurate and consistent FTE calculations across all departments.

- Use FTE data to inform budgeting, staffing, and resource allocation decisions.

- Regularly review and update FTE calculations to reflect changes in workforce or business strategy.

- Educate all levels of management on the importance of FTE in operational and strategic decisions.

This structured approach to defining and using FTE within a company not only aids in operational efficiency but also supports strategic business planning and financial management.
