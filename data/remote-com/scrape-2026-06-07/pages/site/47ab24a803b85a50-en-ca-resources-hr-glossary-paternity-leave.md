# Paternity leave

## What is paternity leave?

Paternity leave is a period of paid or unpaid time off granted to a father or non-birthing parent after the birth, adoption, or surrogacy of a child. It allows new parents to bond with their child, support the birthing parent, and adjust to family life during a critical transition period.

Depending on the country and company policy, paternity leave may be offered as a legal right, a discretionary benefit, or part of a broader parental leave policy.

## How does paternity leave work?

The structure of paternity leave varies based on local laws and organisational policies:

- Length: Paternity leave can range from a few days to several weeks or months. Some countries mandate a minimum duration.

- Paid vs. unpaid: Some governments or employers offer fully or partially paid leave; others provide only unpaid time off.

- Eligibility: Leave may be limited to full-time employees, require a minimum length of service, or apply only in specific family situations (e.g., birth vs. adoption).

- Timing: Paternity leave can take effect immediately after the child’s arrival or within a specific window.

## Why do companies offer paternity leave?

Providing paternity leave supports family wellbeing and promotes workplace equity. It helps companies:

- Encourage gender equality in caregiving responsibilities.

- Improve retention and job satisfaction among new parents.

- Support work-life balance and mental health.

- Comply with labour laws or enhance employer brand through competitive benefits.

## Examples of paternity leave in practice

- A company offers two weeks of fully paid paternity leave to all new fathers after birth or adoption.

- A father uses five days of statutory paternity leave immediately after the birth, followed by two weeks of holiday time.

- A global employer aligns paternity leave policies with local laws across different countries while offering a minimum global baseline benefit.

## Paternity leave vs. parental leave or maternity leave

Paternity, maternity, and parental leave serve different roles in supporting new parents. Here’s how each term differ:

- Paternity leave: Time off specifically for the father or non-birthing parent.

- Maternity leave: Leave provided to the birthing parent, often longer and sometimes medically required.

- Parental leave: A broader term that may apply to either parent, often used in gender-neutral or inclusive policies.

Some companies offer shared parental leave, allowing parents to split or transfer leave entitlements.

## Things to consider with paternity leave

Employers and employees should keep in mind:

- National and local laws that define minimum entitlements and job protections.

- Internal policies that expand beyond legal requirements.

- The cultural and social norms around parenthood and caregiving in different regions.

- Coordination with HR and payroll to track leave duration, pay, and benefits continuity.
