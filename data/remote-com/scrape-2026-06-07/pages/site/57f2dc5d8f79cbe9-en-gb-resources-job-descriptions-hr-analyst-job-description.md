An HR analyst assesses workforce data to inform HR policies, and uses statistical tools to forecast trends and advise on strategic planning in areas like employee retention, and compensation.

## About the HR analyst role

As our HR analyst, you’d be responsible for analysing workforce data and HR processes to recommend strategies that improve employee performance and satisfaction.

## HR analyst responsibilities

- Data analysis and reporting: A HR analyst is responsible for collecting and analysing employee data to identify trends, forecast needs, and measure the effectiveness of HR policies and initiatives.

- Performance management: They assist in the development and implementation of performance management systems, ensuring they are fair, consistent, and aligned with organisational goals.

- Benefits administration: HR analysts help design and administer employee benefits programs, such as health insurance, retirement plans, and wellness programs, ensuring they meet employee needs and comply with legal requirements.

- Recruitment and hiring: They support the recruitment process by developing job descriptions, screening candidates, and analysing recruitment metrics to improve hiring processes.

- Employee relations: HR analysts may also be involved in resolving workplace conflicts, conducting employee satisfaction surveys, and working with management to address employee concerns.

- Training and development: They play a key role in identifying training needs and designing training programs to enhance employee skills and promote career development within the organisation.

## HR analyst qualifcations

- Bachelor’s degree: Possess a bachelor's degree in human resources, business administration, psychology, or a related field; advanced degrees or HR certifications are preferred.

- HR experience: Experience in human resources, with a strong background in HR analytics or a related area.

- Analytical skills: Demonstrated ability to perform complex data analysis and provide actionable insights.

- Technical proficiency: Proficient in HRIS, advanced Excel skills, and familiarity with data visualisation tools such as Tableau or Power BI.

- Communication skills: Excellent communication abilities, capable of effectively presenting data insights and collaborating with various stakeholders.

- Legal compliance: Knowledge of employment laws and regulations, ensuring all HR practices adhere to legal standards.

## HR analyst skills

- Data analysis: Skilled in interpreting and leveraging complex data.

- Technical proficiency: Proficient with HR software and data visualisation tools.

- Communication: Able to clearly convey insights and data findings.

- Attention to detail: Ensures accuracy in data reporting and analysis.

- Regulatory knowledge: Understands applicable labour laws and HR regulations.

Becoming a HR analyst typically involves pursuing a degree in human resources, business administration, or a related field, along with courses in data analysis and statistics. It's also crucial to gain practical experience through internships or entry-level HR positions, to develop the necessary skills and understanding of HR systems and practices.

### Career progression for a HR analyst

A HR analyst can advance to senior analyst roles, where they handle more complex data projects and strategic planning. With experience, they may also move into HR management or specialised roles such as a compensation and benefits manager, contributing to broader organisational development.
