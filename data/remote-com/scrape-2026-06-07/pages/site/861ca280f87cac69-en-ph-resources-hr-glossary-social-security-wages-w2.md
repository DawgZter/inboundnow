# What are social security wages on w2?

## What Social Security wages on Form W-2 mean for employees

Social Security wages refer to the earnings reported on an employee’s Form W-2 that are subject to Social Security tax. This amount is important because it determines the Social Security tax withheld from the employee, the employer’s matching contribution, and it helps establish the benefits the employee may be eligible to receive upon retirement. Certain types of income are excluded from this calculation—for example, contributions to a 401(k) plan.

## How Social Security wages on the W-2 form are calculated

Below is a clear breakdown of how Social Security wages are shown and computed on Form W-2:

Location on Form W-2 . Social Security wages appear in box 3 of Form W-2. That box displays the total wages paid to the employee during the year that are subject to Social Security tax.

Tax rate and limit. For 2023, the Social Security tax rate for employees is 6.2%, with employers matching that amount for a combined rate of 12.4%. There is an annual wage cap: for 2023 the limit is $160,200, so earnings above that figure are not subject to Social Security tax.

What’s included? Social Security wages generally cover most earned income, such as salaries, wages, and bonuses. Note that certain forms of compensation—like contributions to a 401(k) plan—are included in Social Security wages even if they may be excluded from federal income tax.

Differences from gross wages . The figure reported in box 3 (Social Security wages) may differ from the amount in box 1 (wages, tips, other compensation) on Form W-2. That discrepancy commonly results from payroll deductions—such as 401(k) contributions or health insurance premiums—that are exempt from federal income taxation.

## Best practices for managing Social Security wages

Employers must accurately track and report the employee earnings that are subject to Social Security tax to ensure compliance with federal law and to protect employees’ future benefits.

HR and payroll teams should:

- Monitor the Social Security wage cap—the annual maximum earnings subject to Social Security tax. This cap is adjusted each year to reflect inflation and other economic influences.

- Keep payroll staff apprised of legislative changes related to Social Security wages; regular training and timely updates will help maintain payroll accuracy and regulatory compliance.

- Adopt robust payroll systems that update automatically with current laws to calculate and report Social Security wages accurately. Doing so reduces the risk of errors and supports compliance.

## Important considerations for companies

- Ensure payroll systems are accurate to avoid penalties and to safeguard employees’ benefits.

- Monitor annual changes to the Social Security wage cap.

- Be transparent with employees about how their Social Security wages are calculated and reported.
