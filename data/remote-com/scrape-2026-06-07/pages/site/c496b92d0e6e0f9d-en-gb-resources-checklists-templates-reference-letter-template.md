# Employee reference letter template

In HR, paperwork rarely tells the full story. That’s where a reference letter can help — a brief but powerful document that confirms an employee’s role, work ethic, and overall character.
Whether you’re responding to a background check, helping a team member secure housing, or supporting a visa application, this flexible reference letter template will help you respond quickly and professionally.

## What is an employee reference letter?

An employee reference letter — sometimes called an employment reference or professional reference — is a short written statement from an employer that confirms the details of someone’s job history and conduct.
An employee reference letter is more factual than persuasive, and is often used for administrative or legal purposes. These letters are typically:

- Brief and neutral in tone

- Focused on job title, dates of employment, and overall performance

- Reused for multiple scenarios

Reference letters are commonly requested as part of:

- Pre-employment checks

- Housing or mortgage applications

- Visa and immigration processes

- Internal audits or HR documentation

They’re also a helpful way to close out the offboarding process on a positive note, especially for high-performing employees who may one day return or refer others.

## Reference letter template (copy and customise)

Below is a flexible, ready-to-use employee reference letter template. You can adapt it to suit any former or current team member, depending on the request.
Simply fill in the brackets with the relevant details, make small adjustments to reflect your working relationship, and you’re good to go.
Pro tip: If you don’t know the name of the recipient, or if the letter is intended for multiple uses, address it to “To whom it may concern.” If the letter is going to a specific person or organisation, customise the salutation.

### Template: Reference letter for former employees

[Your full name]
[Your job title]
[Company name]
[Company address] (optional)
[Email address]
[Phone number] (optional)
[Date]
To whom it may concern,
This is to confirm that [employee full name] was employed at [company name] from [start date] to [end date], most recently in the role of [job title].
During their time with us, [employee first name] was a dependable and professional member of the team. They were responsible for [brief overview of responsibilities or department], and consistently demonstrated strengths such as [insert general traits — e.g., adaptability, clear communication, or initiative].
Their contributions were valued across the organisation, and they approached their work with integrity, care, and a collaborative mindset. [He/She/They] concluded their employment in good standing, and we were grateful for the positive impact they had during their time with us.
If you require any further details or verification, please feel free to contact me at [email address/your contact details].
Sincerely,

[Your full name]

### Reference letter for current employees

[Your full name]
[Your job title]
[Company name]
[Company address] (optional)
[Email address]
[Phone number] (optional)
[Date]
To whom it may concern,
This is to confirm that [employee full name] is currently employed at [company name], and has been with us since [start date]. [He/She/They] currently hold the position of [job title], reporting to the [department/team name] team.
Throughout their time with us, [employee first name] has consistently demonstrated qualities such as [insert traits — e.g., reliability, professionalism, and strong communication skills]. They are responsible for [brief overview of responsibilities or key contributions], and continue to be a valued member of our team.
I am confident that [employee first name] brings the same high standards to any responsibility they undertake.
If you need further information or verification, please feel free to contact me at [email address/your contact details].
Sincerely,

[Your full name]

## Best practices for writing a reference letter

Even a short reference can make a strong impression — especially if it’s clear, consistent, and written with care. Here are a few tips:

- Keep it neutral and factual
Reference letters are about verification, not endorsement. Focus on job titles, dates, responsibilities, and overall conduct. Avoid exaggerated praise or subjective opinions.

- Confirm employee consent
Check that the employee has requested the letter and is comfortable with the details you’re sharing. This helps protect privacy and ensures the letter serves its intended purpose.

- Use your letterhead
For formal use cases, such as immigration, housing, or legal requests, using company letterhead or an official PDF adds professionalism and credibility.

- Include contact details
Adding your email (and optionally, your phone number) gives the recipient a way to follow up for verification or clarification if needed.

- Make it professional, but warm
Even if the reference letter is brief, a considerate tone can make a difference, especially when you're acknowledging someone’s contributions to your team.
