## Payroll services in Germany

Germany has a strong economy with key industries in automotive, manufacturing, and finance. With specific labor laws and tax regulations, employers in Germany must ensure compliance with local payroll requirements, including social security contributions, employment contracts, and tax obligations. Understanding these regulations is essential for smooth payroll operations and legal compliance.

### Payroll breakdown in Germany

Employers in Germany must adhere to national payroll regulations regarding wages, taxes, and social contributions. Below is an overview of key payroll components:

### Minimum wage and working hours

- Minimum wage: Germany’s minimum wage is EUR 12.00 per hour.

- Payroll frequency: Salaries are generally paid monthly.

- Standard working hours: The normal workweek in Germany is 40 hours, typically spread over five days.

- Overtime: Employees are entitled to additional pay for overtime work, calculated based on labor regulations and collective agreements.

### Taxation and social security contributions

- Personal income tax: Germany has a progressive income tax system with rates ranging from 0% to 45% depending on income levels.

- Employer contributions:

- Social security: 19.375% of the gross salary.

- Employee contributions:

- Social security: 19.375% of the gross salary.

- Corporate tax: The corporate income tax rate in Germany is 15% plus a solidarity surcharge.

- Tax reporting: Employers must file payroll taxes and contributions with the German tax authorities regularly.

### Payroll compliance in Germany

- Employment contracts must be provided in writing and specify terms such as salary, working hours, and job responsibilities.

- Payroll deductions: Employers must ensure accurate deductions for social security and income tax.

- Employers must stay updated on changes to Germany’s labor laws and tax regulations to avoid penalties.
